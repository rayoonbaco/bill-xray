# Product Spec — Bill X-Ray V1

## One-line promise
**Thousands of pages become five things worth knowing. Every consequential claim traces back to the bill.**

## Hero
BILL X-RAY
See what they actually wrote.

## Primary action
SELECT A BILL -> X-RAY THIS BILL

## Five visible panels
1. What It Really Does
2. Follow the Money
3. Barrel Scan
4. Who Wins / Who Pays / Who Gets Power
5. Left | Right | Text

## Evidence drawer
Click any citation to show:
- exact source location;
- bill text excerpt/location;
- plain-English translation;
- why it was classified this way;
- claim class;
- confidence;
- cross-reference chain if needed.

## Trust language
- Source-first analysis
- No citation, no claim
- Same rubric, every party
- Interpretation is labeled

## Non-goals for V1
- No user accounts
- No social feed
- No automatic accusations
- No giant policy encyclopedia
- No Rosetta Stone generalization
- No 50 fully analyzed bills before the first 2 work perfectly

## Deferred post-V1 bill discovery
After the two-bill proving ground is trustworthy, add a search-first bill discovery path without replacing the curated catalog. A user can enter a keyword or phrase, see a dropdown of likely bill matches with enough context to disambiguate them, select a bill, ingest its official source into the same evidence pipeline, and run the identical five-panel analysis. This is explicitly deferred until the V1 pass sequence is complete; it must reuse the same ingestion, citation, translation, specialist, referee, and validation contracts rather than create a second analysis path.

## One-glance layout doctrine
The public surface should feel calm, balanced, and immediately legible. On a normal desktop viewport, the bill report should fit on one screen whenever the five-panel content can do so without hiding evidence or shrinking text below comfortable reading size. Use modest type, balanced whitespace, restrained accent colors, consistent card geometry, and strong visual hierarchy. Mobile may scroll; desktop should not require scrolling merely because of oversized typography or decorative spacing.

## Pass 14 synthesis rule

The five panels are fixed, but bullet counts are ceilings, not quotas. Never manufacture a claim to fill visual space. A specialty panel may show a neutral no-finding state when nothing appropriate survives the referee. Panel 5 remains exactly LEFT | RIGHT | TEXT for a verified report.

## Evidence interaction (Pass 15)

A verified claim exposes one compact **Evidence** control. It opens a side drawer over the existing report, verifies the stored citation anchor against the canonical source, and shows the exact source location and text. An official-source link is offered when retained in provenance. The reader can close the drawer in one action and remain on the five-panel summary.

## Pass 17 proving-ground rule

The two demo bills use one shared operational standard: official public-law acquisition, local source fingerprinting, the same 14-stage evidence chain, visible stage progress, durable checkpoints, and honest draft status when the record does not earn a complete public report. Long processing time must never be indistinguishable from a frozen process.

## Pass 18 release gate

The public five-panel synthesis is now followed by a political-bias and selection-quality red team. The audit does not change source evidence. It tests whether the synthesis chose material, readable, politically symmetric claims and can place a release on hold when selection quality fails.

## Search-any-bill launch requirement (Pass 22)
Search is an identity-resolution layer, not an evidence shortcut. Users choose an exact GovInfo congressional bill package/version. The selected official text is stored locally and analyzed through the normal citation, skeptic, referee, red-team, and audit gates. Different bill versions remain distinct. Verified results are cached by source fingerprint.
